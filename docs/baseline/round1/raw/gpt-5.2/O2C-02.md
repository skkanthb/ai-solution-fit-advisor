## Prompt

```
I'm an enterprise architect scoping an AI solution for this ERP / supply chain use case:

Credit hold release: Orders blocked by credit checks wait in a queue while credit analysts review each one, delaying shipment for customers who would have paid.

What AI approach and models would you recommend?
```

## Answer

### Recommended AI approach: “Decision support + automation” for credit-hold release  
For this use case, the highest-value pattern is **AI triage and recommendation**, with **policy-guardrailed auto-release** for low-risk cases and **human-in-the-loop** review for the rest. In practice this is a **hybrid of predictive ML + rules + an LLM** for explanation/workflow—rather than an LLM alone.

---

## 1) Target operating model (what the AI should do)
**Goal:** reduce queue time without increasing bad debt.

1) **Ingest** order + customer + AR + payment + credit exposure + dispute/returns + shipment history + external signals (optional).  
2) **Score each held order** for:
   - probability of non-payment / delinquency
   - expected loss (risk × exposure)
   - “collectability” / likely payment date
   - recommended action (release, partial release, ask for prepay, escalate)  
3) **Route**:
   - **Auto-release** if low risk and within policy tolerances
   - **Analyst review** if medium/high risk or policy exceptions
   - **Auto-request** actions (e.g., prepayment, updated PO, credit limit change request)
4) **Explain** the recommendation with auditable reasons and relevant evidence.
5) **Learn** from outcomes (paid on time? DSO? write-offs? analyst overrides?).

---

## 2) Core modeling approach (predictive + causal-ish + optimization)
### A. Supervised risk model (primary)
**What it predicts:** probability of delinquency/default over a horizon (e.g., 30/60/90 days past due) given releasing this order.

**Model families to consider**
- **Gradient-boosted decision trees** (recommended baseline):  
  - *XGBoost / LightGBM / CatBoost*  
  - Strengths: high performance on tabular ERP data, handles missingness, good calibration options, interpretable with SHAP, easier governance than deep nets.
- **Regularized logistic regression** as a transparent benchmark (and sometimes production choice for governance).
- **Neural nets** only if you have very large data volumes and complex patterns; usually not necessary for ERP credit data.

**Why this matters:** credit hold release is fundamentally a **tabular risk scoring** problem.

### B. Expected loss / utility model (decision layer)
A probability alone isn’t enough; you want an action. Add:
- **Exposure at risk** (order value, outstanding AR, credit limit utilization, margin)
- **Cost of delay** (late shipment penalties, churn risk proxy, expediting cost)
- **Expected loss** = PD × LGD × EAD (adapted to your context)

Then choose action via **policy + optimization**:
- “Release if expected loss < threshold AND within policy constraints”
- “Partial release up to X if exposure utilization is high”
- “Require prepayment if PD high but customer strategic”

This can be implemented as a **decision engine** (rules + thresholds) fed by ML outputs.

### C. Time-to-pay / delinquency timing (optional but valuable)
For customers who do pay but late, a **survival model** can predict *when* cash arrives:
- **Cox proportional hazards**, **Random Survival Forests**, or gradient-boosted survival variants.
- Useful for deciding between “release now” vs “hold until payment clears” for borderline cases.

### D. Anomaly + entity resolution (risk controls)
- **Unsupervised anomaly detection** (Isolation Forest, robust z-scores) for:
  - sudden order size spikes
  - ship-to changes
  - unusual payment behavior shifts
- **Graph/entity features** (optional): connect parent/subsidiary accounts, shared addresses, common payers—often improves risk signals.

---

## 3) Where an LLM fits (not for the score)
Use an LLM for **workflow acceleration and explainability**, not as the primary risk model.

### LLM use cases
- **Case summarization**: “Why is this order on hold? What changed?”
- **Evidence retrieval (RAG)** from policies, credit notes, emails, dispute notes, contract terms.
- **Recommendation narrative**: generate an analyst-ready justification with cited factors (from the ML model + retrieved policy text).
- **Analyst copilot**: draft customer outreach (“request remittance advice”, “confirm PO”, “offer split shipment with prepay”).

### Recommended LLM choices
- Enterprise-grade LLM with strong tool-use / function-calling:
  - **OpenAI GPT-4.1 / GPT-4o** (strong reasoning + tool calling)
  - **Azure OpenAI equivalents** if you’re in Microsoft ecosystem
  - **Claude** (strong summarization) or **Gemini** depending on your vendor alignment
- Keep it **grounded** with:
  - RAG over internal policy + customer correspondence
  - Strict instruction hierarchy (“do not invent policy; cite sources”)
  - Structured outputs (JSON) for audit logs

---

## 4) Features that usually drive performance (ERP/SCM specific)
Typical high-signal features:
- Payment history: DSO, days past due distribution, # of broken promises, last payment date, payment method
- AR profile: open invoice aging buckets, dispute counts, unapplied cash
- Credit exposure: utilization %, recent limit changes, override history
- Order/shipping: order amount vs historical, product category risk, shipment terms, region
- Customer behavior: cancellation/return rate, claim rate
- Operational signals: customer service escalations, recent master data changes (address/bank), new ship-to
- Macro/external (optional): business registry events, credit bureau scores, industry downturn signals

---

## 5) Governance, risk, and controls (important for production)
Because this affects revenue and credit losses, design for:
- **Calibration and monitoring** (PD drift, segment drift, override rates)
- **Explainability**: SHAP for tree models + “reason codes”
- **Fairness/compliance**: avoid protected-class proxies; document feature use
- **Human-in-the-loop**: thresholds + random sampling audits + override capture
- **Champion/challenger**: maintain a conservative baseline model in parallel
- **Outcome definition**: define “bad outcome” carefully (e.g., 60+ DPD within 90 days of shipment)

---

## 6) Practical model stack recommendation (what I’d implement first)
### Phase 1 (8–12 weeks): High ROI foundation
1) **LightGBM/XGBoost PD model** (binary: delinquent vs not within horizon)  
2) **Decision policy layer** (rules + thresholds using PD and exposure)  
3) **SHAP-based reason codes** surfaced in the credit workbench  
4) **LLM copilot** for summarization + policy Q&A (RAG), *not* for scoring  
5) **Auto-release** only for the lowest-risk band with tight guardrails

### Phase 2: Improve decisions and automation
- Add **survival/time-to-pay**
- Add **anomaly detection** + master data change alerts
- Add **partial shipment optimization**
- Add **learning from analyst overrides** (but be careful—overrides can encode bias)

---

## 7) Metrics to prove value
- Queue time reduction / median time-to-release
- Increase in on-time shipment rate
- Bad debt / write-off rate (should not worsen beyond tolerance)
- DSO impact
- Analyst productivity: cases/day, time/case
- Override rate and post-override outcomes
- “Auto-release band” performance (precision/recall vs losses)

---

## 8) Key scoping questions (to choose thresholds and model horizon)
1) What is the definition of “non-payment” (e.g., 60+ DPD, write-off, collection)?  
2) Typical order cycle time and horizon (30/60/90 days)?  
3) Are holds triggered only by limit/utilization, or also disputes, master data, sanctions, etc.?  
4) Do you have enough labeled outcomes (>= 12–24 months history) and stable customer identifiers?  
5) What is your acceptable risk tolerance for auto-release (e.g., expected loss cap per month)?
